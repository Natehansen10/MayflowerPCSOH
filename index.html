The Build File

Most builders hand you a brochure. We'd rather hand you the file.

You're standing in a home we finished this year. Everything in it was a decision — about structure, about systems, about where the money went and what it bought. Eight of those decisions are marked with a placard as you walk through, and all eight are open to read here.

How this works
  1. Walk the home. Eight placards, numbered 01 to 08, in the order you'll pass them.
  2. Open the detail. Scan the code on the placard, or tap it in the list below. Each one takes about a minute to read.
  3. Save what matters to you. One tap on any detail. No sign-in, no email.
  4. Plan your build before you leave. Seven questions, about four minutes, and the answer appears on this phone.
The eight details

What each one covers, and whether it carries a number.

    Saving a detail keeps it on this phone only. It travels with you into your build brief at the end.

    Before you leave

    What would a home like this cost you?

    Seven questions — where you're building, how big, what level of finish, what the ground looks like, where you are with a lot, when you'd start, and who we're talking to. About four minutes.

    The answer appears on your screen straight away: a preliminary cost range for the home you described, a design-to-move-in schedule, and a month-by-month picture of when the money is actually spent. No email required to see it. If you want the written version, you can ask for it at the end.

    It is a planning range, not a bid, and we say so on the page. It is still more than most builders will tell you before a contract.

    Start the seven questions
    Who built this

    Two owners. Every project.

    Mayflower is Curtis and Dave. We build a small number of custom homes at a time in the Wasatch Back, most of them in Skyridge, and one of us is on your job every week for the whole of it. There is no project manager between you and the person who signed your contract.

    That's the whole pitch. We keep the volume low because a home at this level does not survive being someone's fourth priority.

    How we work

    Open book, on purpose

    You see every subcontractor bid we receive. You see our fee as its own line. Allowances are set against real quotes for things you've actually chosen, not against a number that makes the contract look smaller than the house.

    When something goes wrong — and on a sixteen-month build something always does — you hear it from us the day it happens, with what it costs and what we recommend. Not on the next draw.

    If you're serious

    Walk a lot with us

    The cheapest hour anyone spends on a custom home is the one standing on dirt with a builder who will tell them the truth. If you own a lot, we'll walk it and give you a real range before you spend a dollar on design. If you don't own one yet, walk it with us before you buy.

    No charge, no obligation, and we'll tell you if we think the lot is a mistake.
